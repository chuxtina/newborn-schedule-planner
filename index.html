One honest caveat. Newborns don't follow schedules — they follow hunger and sleep pressure. The strongest evidence supports responsive (cue-based) feeding in the first months, not rigid clocks (AAP, 2022). Treat these schedules as a loose frame for your day, not a prescription. The most useful number on this page is the daily milk target; the times around it are just one plausible way to distribute it.

Where these numbers come from

Every claim is tied to a primary source or major guideline. Evidence quality is labeled: Strong = multiple peer-reviewed studies or major-body guideline; Moderate = single large study or consistent practitioner consensus with some empirical support; Practitioner consensus = widely taught, limited rigorous evidence.

  • Kent JC et al. (2006). "Volume and frequency of breastfeedings and fat content of breast milk throughout the day." Pediatrics, 117(3), e387–e395. Source for the 478–1356 mL/day range and ~788 mL/day mean intake in exclusively breastfed babies 1–6 months. Strong
  • American Academy of Pediatrics (2022). "Policy Statement: Breastfeeding and the Use of Human Milk." Pediatrics, 150(1). 8–12 feeds per 24h for breastfed newborns; cue-based / responsive feeding. Strong
  • AAP HealthyChildren.org. "Amount and Schedule of Formula Feedings." Formula volumes by age and the 2.5 oz × weight-in-lbs/day rule of thumb. Strong
  • Hirshkowitz M et al. (2015). "National Sleep Foundation's sleep time duration recommendations." Sleep Health, 1(1), 40–43. 14–17 hours/day recommended sleep for newborns 0–3 months. Strong
  • Iglowstein I et al. (2003). "Sleep duration from infancy to adolescence: reference values and generational trends." Pediatrics, 111(2), 302–307. Reference values: ~14.8h total at 1 month (8.7h night, 6.1h day); ~14.2h at 3 months. Strong
  • AAP Section on Council on Communications and Media + AAP tummy time guidance. "Back to Sleep, Tummy to Play." Short tummy-time sessions multiple times daily from birth, building to ~15–30 min/day total by ~7 weeks. Strong
  • Kent JC et al. (2008). "Importance of vacuum for breastmilk expression." Breastfeeding Medicine, 3(1), 11–19. Pump output and frequency for establishing and maintaining supply. Moderate
  • Mohrbacher N. (2020). Breastfeeding Answers (2nd ed.). Lactation reference compiled from research; basis for "milk comes in" timing and exclusive-pumping cadence. Moderate
  • Wake-window guidance (0–3 months). Synthesis from pediatric sleep practitioners (Mindell, West, Karp) and observational data. 30–90 min wake windows are widely taught and clinically consistent, but the empirical literature is thin. Use as a soft signal, not a rule. Practitioner consensus
  • "Eat-Play-Sleep" structure. Hogg T. (2001), The Baby Whisperer; Babywise-adjacent literature. Popular framework. No randomized evidence shows superior outcomes for newborns vs. responsive feeding; some lactation researchers caution it can suppress milk supply in early weeks if applied rigidly. Included as one option, not a recommendation. Practitioner consensus
